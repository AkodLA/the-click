# PITCH: facts we can say on stage

Rule from `PLAN.md`: **no source = we don't say it.** Every number below has a source checked on 7 Nov 2026.
If you add a fact, add the link and the date you checked it. Owner: T3 (facts), T14 (script).

## The 3 facts to use

| # | Say this (English) | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **"More than 1 in 6 people in Norway are immigrants: almost one million."** (987 120 immigrants, 17.5 % of the population, plus 238 507 born in Norway to immigrant parents.) | SSB, key figures on immigration, 2026: https://www.ssb.no/innvandring-og-innvandrere/nokkeltall |
| 2 | **"Since 2022 it's against the law for public services to use children as interpreters."** Tolkeloven § 4, in force 1 Jan 2022. Exceptions: emergencies, or when it's justifiable for the child and the situation. | Tolkeloven summary (NHI): https://nhi.no/rettigheter-og-helsetjeneste/pasientrettigheter/tolkeloven |
| 3 | **"Norway's public services ordered over 1.4 million interpreting jobs in 2025, and about 1 in 4 was done by someone without interpreter qualifications."** (74 % by qualified interpreters.) | IMDi, *Tolkemonitor LOV 2025* (June 2026), § 4 and § 4.2: https://www.imdi.no/globalassets/rapporter/tolk/20260610-tolkemonitor-lov-2025_rev.pdf |

### Supporting (qualitative, no number)
- IMDi's 2025 report refers to a 2026 study (Proba samfunnsanalyse and Agenda Kaupang) finding that **young people are still widely used as language helpers**, in meetings and in written messages, often outside the law's exceptions. Say: *"Kids still end up interpreting for their parents."* Don't put a number on it. Same source as fact 3, § 3.1.1.

## How to use them in the 2-minute pitch
- **Hook (0:00–0:15):** fact 1. "Almost a million people in Norway learned Norwegian from a textbook. Then they meet real Norwegians."
- **Tolk (1:25–1:45):** fact 2, then the supporting point, then fact 3. "The law says no kids as interpreters. Reality: they still do it, and there aren't enough interpreters. Tolk is the bridge until you speak Norwegian yourself."

## Do NOT say (no source, or not true)
- ❌ "Duolingo/apps don't teach spoken Norwegian": no study. Say instead: *"Most apps teach textbook sentences; we teach when to say what."*
- ❌ Any % of immigrants who "struggle with Norwegian". The SSB survey we found is from 2016 and doesn't measure that.
- ❌ "Certified interpreter" or "replaces interpreters". Tolk is a **communication aid**, not a qualified interpreter (legal note, see PLAN.md).
- ❌ "Our placement test gives your CEFR level". Say *"estimates your level"*: it's 9 scored questions.

## Q&A prep: if a juror asks
- *"Where's the number from?"* Name the source (SSB / tolkeloven / IMDi Tolkemonitor) and the year.
- *"Is your Norwegian content correct?"* Every phrase card is checked by a native speaker on the team before the demo (see the note at the top of `src/lib/content/phrases.ts`).
- *"Isn't Tolk breaking tolkeloven?"* ⚠️ Careful, a lawyer is on the jury. The law puts the duty on **public bodies** (schools, NAV, health): when they need an interpreter, they must use a qualified one. So in a parent-teacher meeting the **school** should book a qualified interpreter. Honest answer: *"Tolk is the parent's own aid for everyday moments (a quick chat at pickup, a message from school) and a backup, not a replacement for the interpreter the school must provide."* Consider changing the demo story from a formal meeting to an informal moment.
